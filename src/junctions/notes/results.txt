To assess whether somatic PRNP cDNA insertions were present in the targeted sequencing libraries, a phenomenon which has been reported for APP in Alzheimer's disease [44], but has since been called into question [51], we re aligned PRNP region reads from 26 CJD and 6 control brains to a set of synthetic PRNP exon1–exon2 junction reference sequences and quantified support for junction spanning fragments.
Across the 32 samples, the number of unique PRNP window fragments ranged from 2,768 to 293,137 per sample (median 100,061). For each sample, thousands of read ends aligned to the junction index, but the majority either failed to map to the PRNP junction contigs or remained unmapped. After restricting to primary, non duplicate, non supplementary alignments to the PRNP junction contigs, several hundred to several thousand alignments per sample remained. Approximately half of these were discarded for low mapping quality (MAPQ < 20). Among the high MAPQ subset, a proportion of reads mapped entirely within one exon, and the remainder crossed the exon1–exon2 junction but failed the prespecified requirement of ≥10 bp aligned on each side of the junction. Consequently, no read fragment in any CJD or control sample satisfied the combined criteria of (i) mapping uniquely to a PRNP exon1–exon2 junction, (ii) MAPQ ≥ 20 and (iii) ≥10 bp overhang on both sides of the junction. Thus, this analysis provides no evidence for recurrent or high frequency PRNP exon1–exon2 junctions in genomic DNA in either CJD or control brains.
